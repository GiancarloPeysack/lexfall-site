What to film: the card is the content

Lexfall is built to be filmed. The daily word card, the definition, the example sentence, the streak, the widget on your lock screen: each is a clean, five-second visual that works as a native short. You do not need to be on camera. The two formats that work:

Faceless. Screen-record the app, or lay text-on-screen word cards over a quiet trending audio. Let the words carry it.

Talking. Read a script below to camera. Rough beats polish here. Speed and honesty win.

The one rule that decides everything: the first three seconds. Lead with the hook, never an introduction. Every script below is written hook-first for exactly this reason. Post the same cut to TikTok, Reels and Shorts. Shorts is worth the extra upload because it keeps ranking in search for months.

The hook library: five scripts, ready to record

Every word below is real Lexfall content. Read them straight, or make them yours.

General · broadest appeal · ~25s

"English has four words for how much someone talks. Knowing them makes you sound razor-sharp."

  • garrulous: talks too much, about nothing. "a garrulous passenger you can't escape on a flight."
  • taciturn: barely speaks. "the famously taciturn director who lets his films talk."
  • laconic: few words, but they land. "a laconic one-word reply."
  • prolix: goes on and on in writing. "so prolix the point gets buried."

Close: "One word like this a day, on your lock screen. Lexfall."

Business · sound senior · ~25s

"Stop saying 'we're low on cash.' Here is how people who run companies say it."

  • liquidity: how fast assets turn into cash. "a run of late payments left them short of liquidity."
  • leverage: using borrowed money to amplify returns. "high leverage magnified both the gains and the losses."
  • collateral: an asset pledged to secure a loan. "the bank took the warehouse as collateral."

Close: "Business English that makes you sound senior. Lexfall."

Medical / OET · nurse audience · ~30s

"If you are a nurse taking the OET: it is not testing your medicine. It is testing this."

  • take a history: systematically gather a patient's story. "before I examine you, I'd like to take a full history."
  • presenting complaint: the main problem, in the patient's own words.
  • open-ended question: one they cannot answer yes or no. "tell me more about that."

Close: "The medical English the ward and the exam expect. Lexfall, choose Medicine."

Emotional hook · ~20s

"There is a single word for a feeling too vast to describe. Most native speakers don't know it."

  • ineffable: too great or sacred to be put into words. "the ineffable quality of grief."
  • sanguine: cheerfully optimistic in a hard situation. "sanguine about the firm's long-term prospects."

Close: "The words you didn't know you were missing. Lexfall."

Curiosity · ~25s

"Four words people use so you won't notice what they are actually saying."

  • obfuscate: deliberately make something unclear. "jargon to obfuscate the true cost."
  • ostensibly: supposedly, but maybe not really. "he resigned ostensibly for health reasons."
  • perfunctory: done with the bare minimum of care. "a perfunctory nod."
  • notwithstanding: in spite of. "notwithstanding these limitations..."

Close: "Read between the lines. Lexfall."

Your link, and the mistake that loses 80% of clicks

Use your own tracking link in your bio: lexfall.app/get/yourhandle. It redirects to the App Store and credits you.

The mistake almost every creator makes: pasting a raw App Store link in a TikTok or Instagram bio. Those apps open links in their own in-app browser, which cannot launch the App Store, so most people hit a dead screen and give up. Your lexfall.app/get/ link is built to break out of the in-app browser into the phone's real browser, so the App Store actually opens. Always use it, never the raw store URL.

On Android? Lexfall is iPhone-only for now. If your audience is largely on Android, send them to lexfall.app/google-play to join the waitlist, so you still capture them and can convert them at launch.

Do's and don'ts

Do

  • Disclose the partnership: #ad or "paid partner", clearly.

  • Show the app, or real words. Authentic beats polished.

Don't

  • Claim exam results or a passing score. Lexfall teaches vocabulary, not the exam.
  • Suggest endorsement by any exam body (OET, IELTS, GRE are trademarks of their owners).
  • Promise Android today. It is coming; point Android users to the waitlist.
  • Invent ratings or "loved by thousands" claims.
